Curriculum Vitae (CV)

Background

I grew up in a state-owned Chinese hospital campus where my parents and other physicians lived in employer-provided housing, and I witnessed how medical services transformed from socialist public goods into highly marketized arrangements. After college graduation, I worked as a teacher and manager at one of the world's largest education corporations for four years. I experienced first-hand how the boundary between public and private education shifted, how for-profit education spawned new organizational forms, changed the way teachers worked and exerted tremendous impact on social inequality. In short, my childhood and work experience have sparked my curiosity in understanding how and why education and healthcare privatized, marketized and financialized in the last few decades in China, the U.S. and around the world. Recently I've also developed interests in understanding how China's education, healthcare and digital technology sectors expand globally.

Research

My research centers on political economy, organizations, economic sociology and social stratification, especially where these areas intersect with education, healthcare and digital technology in China, the U.S. and global context. Employing sociological insight and mixed methods, I study schools, hospitals and platforms as organizations, and educators, physicians and tech workers as occupations, as well as how education, healthcare and digital technology sectors impact social inequality and stratification. My book, The Fruits of Opportunism: Noncompliance and the Evolution of China's Supplemental Education Industry, was published by the University of Chicago Press in October 2022. This book draws on the 40 years' emergence and transformation of China's massive supplemental education sector to shed new light on state-market-society relationship, organizational change and China's turn to market capitalism. It won the Honorable Mention of the Asia/Transnational Book Award, American Sociological Association (ASA) in 2023.
