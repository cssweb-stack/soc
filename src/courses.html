Sociology (SOC) Courses

Sociology (SOC) courses at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa invite students to examine human behavior, social institutions, and structural dynamics across local, regional, and global contexts, from Hawaiʻi and the Asia-Pacific to the broader world. Offerings are organized by academic division, from foundational undergraduate coursework to advanced doctoral research.

{% call imageTextRow('/media/shared/placeholders/temp.jpg', 500, 500, sidebarClass="sidebar-left-sm") %}

Lower Division (100–200 Level)

Lower-division coursework develops a sociological perspective through core concepts of social relationships, social structures, and the forces driving everyday life. Courses at this level introduce students to quantitative data analysis, globalization, sustainability, race and ethnic relations, family dynamics, and contemporary social problems such as inequality, deviance, and punishment, establishing essential analytical tools for further study.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/shared/placeholders/temp.jpg', 500, 500, sidebarClass="sidebar-left-sm") %}

Upper Division (300–400 Level)

Upper-division coursework deepens students’ methodological skills and theoretical understanding through rigorous surveys and intensive analytical seminars across major sociological subfields. Students master the principles of sociological inquiry, social statistics, and qualitative field methods while examining specialized topics including classical and modern theory, criminology and law, stratification, demography, urban sociology, education, aging, gender, medical sociology, popular culture, and the social institutions of East Asia and Hawaiʻi. Upper-division coursework also emphasizes applied learning through internships, peer teaching leadership, and faculty-mentored independent research projects.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/shared/placeholders/temp.jpg', 500, 500, sidebarClass="sidebar-left-sm") %}

Graduate Division (600–800 Level)

Graduate-level training in theoretical frameworks, research design, and advanced statistical modeling prepares students for professional sociological scholarship and original knowledge production. Seminars cover classical and contemporary theory, regression and multivariate analysis, qualitative fieldwork, content analysis, evaluation research, and demographic methods, alongside deep dives into medical sociology, mental health, stratification, feminist criminology, social movements, conflict resolution, and comparative studies of Asia, the Pacific Islands, and race and ethnicity, culminating in academic teaching preparation and dedicated thesis and dissertation research.

{% endcall %}
