Why Study Sociology?

Sociology explores questions about why inequalities persist, how communities respond to change, and what shapes health, institutions, and opportunities. At the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa, students learn to examine evidence, question familiar assumptions, and connect individual experiences with broader social patterns. These perspectives help students understand the world around them and bring informed thinking to the issues they care about.

Undergraduate Study

Undergraduate students build a foundation for understanding society and investigating how it works. Through the Sociology minor or BA, students explore social relationships, institutions, inequality, and change while developing research and analytical skills. Coursework connects sociological ideas with topics such as health, crime and law, culture, and the environment. These perspectives and skills support further study and careers in business and industry, community and social services, education, government, the justice system, and research.

Graduate Study

Graduate students develop the theoretical knowledge and research methods to investigate social questions in depth. The MA and PhD programs bring students and faculty together around interests spanning inequality, health and aging, crime and law, globalization and sustainability, and societies across Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific. Students develop their research interests, learn to design and conduct sociological studies, and contribute to understanding the communities and institutions they study.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/shared/placeholders/temp.jpg', 500, 500) %}

Career Directions

Sociological perspectives and research skills can support work in community and social services, government, business, education, and the justice system. Depending on their qualifications and experience, graduates may pursue opportunities in research, program evaluation, policy, human resources, and nonprofit administration. Advanced study can also prepare students for research and teaching careers in colleges and universities.
