Asia, Pacific, and Hawaiʻi in Regional and Global Contexts

Comparative and transnational research connects the sociology of Asia, the Pacific, and Hawaiʻi with regional and global contexts. Research spans Hawaiʻi, Australia, Japan, Indonesia, China, and Korea, with attention to Hawaiʻi within the broader context of Oceania. Topics include family and gender, globalization processes, culture and individual agency, aging and health, law and society, criminal justice, colonialism, organizations and occupations, social stratification and social class, economic development, environmental justice and sustainability, and science and technology.

Related Courses

  • SOC 357: Japanese Society and Culture
  • SOC 358: Sociology of Korea

  • SOC 495: Topics in Sociology

Research Area Faculty

Drawing on area and language expertise in East and Southeast Asia, faculty contribute to the department’s comparative research strengths in Asia, the Pacific, and Hawaiʻi and its longstanding commitment to sociological research in Hawaiʻi within Oceania.
