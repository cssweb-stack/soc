Health, Aging, and Medical Sociology

Health, aging, and medical sociology investigates the social causes and consequences of health, illness, and health care, including aging and the life course. A life course perspective situates health and ill-health at the intersections of power relations involving gender, race and ethnicity, class, and colonialism. Research examines the effects of chronic stress, socioeconomic status, social support, religion, immigrant status, and discrimination on self-rated health and psychological distress among different racial and ethnic groups. It also addresses sociocultural and political influences on medical and nutritional sciences and on medical and nutritional interventions. Medical sociology is an established academic research specialization, represented within the American Sociological Association by a dedicated group of scholars studying health, illness, and health care.
