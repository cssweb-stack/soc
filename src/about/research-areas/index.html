Research Areas

Research in the Department of Sociology at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa connects questions about social relationships and institutions with issues of inequality, health, law, globalization, and sustainability. Five research areas bring these interests into focus, linking the study of Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific with broader regional and global processes.

{% call imageTextRow('/media/units/soc/research-areas/asia-pacific-hawaii-regional-global-contexts.webp', 600, 600) %}

Asia, Pacific, and Hawaiʻi in Regional and Global Contexts

Explores societies in Asia, the Pacific, and Hawaiʻi through comparative and transnational research on family and gender, globalization, health, colonialism, social class, and environmental justice.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/units/soc/research-areas/crime-law-social-control.webp', 600, 600) %}

Crime, Law, and Social Control

Examines the making, breaking, and enforcement of social norms and formal laws, including the social construction of crime and deviance, policing, punishment, and the relationship between law and everyday life.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/units/soc/research-areas/health-aging-medical-sociology.webp', 600, 600) %}

Health, Aging, and Medical Sociology

Investigates the social causes and consequences of health, illness, and health care across the life course, including aging, stress, discrimination, social support, and inequalities in health.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/units/soc/research-areas/nationalism-globalization-sustainability.webp', 600, 600) %}

Nationalism, Globalization, and Sustainability

Studies how global connections and exchanges transform political, cultural, environmental, and organizational life, including nationalism, migration, racism, transnational organizations, and sustainability.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/units/soc/research-areas/race-ethnicity-gender-class.webp', 600, 600) %}

Race, Ethnicity, Gender, and Class

Traces how inequalities organized by race, ethnicity, gender, and class are formed, maintained, and disrupted, including their connections to social justice, immigration, colonialism, health disparities, and ethnic identity.

{% endcall %}
