Crime, Law, and Social Control

The study of crime, law, and social control addresses the making, breaking, and enforcement of rules and the social construction, regulation, and consequences of crime and deviance. Research covers official and unofficial policing, court processing, and punishment, drawing on the fields of Critical Criminology, Punishment and Society, and Law and Society. Critical Criminology examines interpersonally harmful acts and social harms such as sexism, racism, classism, heteronormativity, imperialism, and forms of organized hatred that rigidly divide societies. It also considers how unequal enforcement and application of laws maintain the race, gender, and class status quo. Punishment and Society examines punishment and penal institutions in their social, political, economic, and historical contexts. Law and Society looks beyond law on the books to law and legality in everyday life, tracing the gaps between expectations of what law does and how it operates in practice.
